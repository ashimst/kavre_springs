We thank the reviewer. We accept the central point: *the data demonstrate ward-level structure; they suggest, but do
not prove, that collection practices produced it.* This sentence now frames the Discussion and the Conclusions. The
manuscript calls the result **ward-associated heterogeneity consistent with survey-process effects**. "Survey-team
effect" is no longer used as a demonstrated cause.

New analyses are in `src/robustness.py`, Tables S52–S55, Table 6 (sensitivity), Fig. 11 (robustness) and notebook

### 1. Separate "ward effect" from "observer effect"

**Introduction** (new paragraph) defines the two terms:
- a *ward effect* is between-ward heterogeneity in recorded drying beyond landscape;
- an *observer effect* is a ward effect caused by data collection.

The inventory identifies wards but not enumerators or visit dates. A ward effect can be measured; its cause can only
be constrained.

**Discussion.** The former "Interpretation" paragraph is now "What the ward signal is, and is not". It lists three
candidate causes:
- survey process;
- visit season;
- real ward-level differences in water schemes, abstraction, roads and land management.

It states which observations favour survey process and why each is weaker than it looks:
- the cross-boundary Knox test has low power;
- the Mantel tests have low power;
- narratives can also be shared through community discussion.

**Other changes:**
- Table 2 now heads its column "survey-process effect".
- Table 2 marks with \* the rows that ward-level physical differences could also produce, and notes that no row is
  diagnostic on its own.
- The abstract, conclusions and README are reworded to match.
- Recommendations add a randomised re-visit by a second team, which would identify observer effects directly.

### 2. Define and validate "dried"; season; ponds

**Definition.** A new paragraph, "What 'dried' means" (Sect. 2.2), states what the published data contain. "Dried" is
the questionnaire's source condition as recorded by the resource person. The published data have:
- no operational criterion;
- no discharge;
- no visit date;
- no enumerator ID.

The survey ran from October 2023 to June 2024. We could not obtain the protocol details. The Data in Brief article
and the ICIMOD repository were not retrievable during revision. The analysis is framed as being about *recorded*
drying.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Every ward-level analysis was repeated for four populations: all sources, springs only (521
ponds excluded), perennial only (930 seasonal sources excluded) and perennial springs only (n = 4,477). Results are in
Table 6 and Table S52.

|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

What the sensitivity analysis shows:
- **Ward signal.** It does not depend on ponds or seasonal sources and strengthens without them.
- **Landscape regions.** Their advantage over grids does **not** survive excluding seasonal sources. This is now

- **Across-ward Knox ratio.** It rises above 1 in the subsets, though on 25–63 pairs and not significantly. The Knox

### 4. Inference after rule selection

**Multiplicity at two levels** (Sect. 3.2, Text S5):
- Pooled Fisher tests use BH over all candidate rules per consequent: 66,563 itemsets, giving 15,744 and 18,580
  significant rules.
- Within-ward tests are now formal. A one-sided Mantel–Haenszel test gives a p-value, with BH over the retained rules
  (3,321 / 2,337). A rule is *ward-robust* at q < 0.05.
- Updated shares: 79 % (drying) and 81 % (activity). These were previously 75 % and 77 %, by CI exclusion.

**Selection.** The text now states that within-ward tests follow selection on the same data. The ward-robust share is
a *robustness summary*, not independent confirmation. Only the held-out block replication is independent of
selection; the median rule replicates on all five blocks.

**Mantel and other non-significant results** are reworded throughout:
- "No association was detected"; with 56 wards, only moderate correlations can be excluded.
- The upslope/downslope and across-ward Knox results are reworded the same way.
- Limitations list the low power explicitly.

### 5. Earthquake claims

The section is now explicitly associative and reports two new analyses (Tables S54–S55).

**Variance between wards.** Only 11 % of PGA variance (7 % of aftershock count) lies within wards.

**12 May PGA under adjustment:**

| Adjustment | Coefficient per SD | 95 % interval |
|:---|---:|:---|
| Environment | 0.69 | 0.24–1.03 |
| Environment + 100 MEMs | 0.74 | 0.37–1.04 |

The text explains that ward fixed effects remove almost all exposure contrast. The attenuation is therefore
uninformative: the data can neither confirm nor exclude a within-ward effect. Separating shaking from ward needs
pre-2015 records or a design with within-ward exposure contrast.
